About Bandhavgarh National Park

Kingdom of Tigers

Bandhavgarh lies in the eastern Satpura hills of Madhya Pradesh. It is renowned for having one of the highest tiger densities in India, set around an ancient hilltop fort.

Its name comes from a legend of the Ramayana. It is believed that Lord Rama gave the fort to his brother Lakshman, so Bandhavgarh means “brother’s fort”.

Notified as a national park in 1968, it forms the heart of the Bandhavgarh Tiger Reserve. Tala, Magdhi and Khitauli are the core zones, while Dhamokhar, Johila and Panpatha are the buffer zones.

Dense forests, grasslands and perennial streams provide ideal habitat, and the reserve records more than 47 kinds of mammals and more than 250 bird species.

Best Time to Visit Bandhavgarh

Bandhavgarh’s safari season runs for most of the year apart from the monsoon. Your best time depends on the weather and what you hope to see.

Winter Season

October – February

The park reopens in mid-October after the monsoon. Mornings are cool and the forest is fresh and green.

Ideal for:

Families, photographers and first-time visitors.

Summer Season

March – June

Summer is hot, and animals often visit waterholes more frequently, although sightings are never guaranteed.

Ideal for:

Visitors focused on wildlife observation.

Monsoon Season

July – September

Heavy rain turns the forest green, but safari access is restricted during these months.

Ideal for:

Planning ahead for the next season.

Visitor note: The reserve’s official website gives mid-October to mid-June as the best time to visit. Zone opening dates and safari timings can change, so check the latest official schedule before planning your safari.

Explore the Tourism Zones

Bandhavgarh has three core tourism zones and three buffer zones. Each offers a different landscape and a different safari experience.

Tourism Zone Type Known For Visiting Period
Tala Core The oldest and most famous zone, with striking topography, ancient caves, statues and high tiger visibility Mid-October to mid-June*
Magdhi Core Bamboo thickets and open grasslands, highly productive for tracking big cats Mid-October to mid-June*
Khitauli Core Varied habitats from bamboo to grassland, good for big-cat tracking Mid-October to mid-June*
Dhamokhar, Panpatha and Johila Buffer Peripheral forests and animal corridors with sal and bamboo, lower vehicle density, leopards, dholes and herbivores Mid-October to mid-June*

*Dates are subject to official permissions, weather and safety conditions, and may change. Confirm on the official safari booking portal.
