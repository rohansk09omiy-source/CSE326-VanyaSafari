Safari zones of four Indian national parks

🐅 Tiger: Dhikala, Bijrani, Dhela🐘 Elephant: Dhikala🐻 Sloth bear: Jhirna🦜 Birds: Dhikala, Durga Devi, Sitabani

India’s first national park (1936). Dhikala is reached by overnight forest rest house booking; Dhela and Jhirna stay open year-round.

🐅 Tiger: Zones 1 to 5🐆 Leopard🐾 Striped hyena🐻 Sloth bear🦜 Birds🏰 Ranthambore Fort

Zones 1 to 5 are core, with the highest tiger density and the lake and ruin scenery. Zones 6 to 10 are buffer: quieter, and better for leopards, hyenas, sloth bears and birds. Icons in the buffer show the typical species mix, not a zone-by-zone count.

🦏 One-horned rhino: Bagori, Kohora🐘 Elephant rides: Bagori, Kohora🐃 Wild water buffalo: Agoratoli, Kohora🦜 Waterbirds: Agoratoli

Kaziranga is split into ranges rather than core and buffer tourist labels. Core area is roughly 482 km² and buffer roughly 548 km². Ranges run west to east along the Brahmaputra.

🐅 Tiger: Tala, Magdhi, Khitauli🐆 Leopard🐕 Dhole (wild dog)🦌 Herbivores🏰 Bandhavgarh Fort

Tala is the oldest and most famous core zone, with the highest tiger visibility. Dhamokhar, Panpatha and Johila are buffer forests that act as animal corridors. Buffer icons show the typical species mix, not a zone-by-zone count.

All four maps are schematic: zone positions and shapes are approximate and not to scale. For navigation or booking, use the park’s official zone map.
